# Idea validation procedure

## Frame the decision

State the proposed customer, user, buyer, painful job, current workaround, promised outcome, geography/segment, time horizon, and recommendation threshold. List the five assumptions most likely to reverse the decision.

## Evidence map

For each assumption, seek support and disconfirmation. Every material market, pricing, funding, traction, competitor, customer, and distribution claim carries a publication date, retrieval date/time, source URI, and primary/secondary label:

- customer count/proxy, urgency, frequency, budget owner, and switching trigger;
- direct competitors, adjacent tools, services, spreadsheets, internal builds, and doing nothing;
- price points, packaging, procurement friction, and likely unit-economics constraints;
- distribution channel, acquisition motion, trust requirement, and plausible first ten customers;
- observable demand: payment, preorder, signed commitment, repeated use, retention, or costly workaround;
- differentiation, defensibility, why now, and unfair access/asset advantage;
- regulatory, technical, concentration, platform, adoption, and execution risks.

Separate observed facts from inference. Search specifically for complaints, failed companies, churn reasons, adverse economics, and incumbent responses.

## Cheapest next experiment

Design the lowest-cost ethical **real-world behavioral** action that could materially change the recommendation. Define the riskiest assumption, target segment, artifact/action, metric, pass/fail threshold, sample/time box, cost, evidence capture, and stop rule. Examples include a problem interview with behavior questions, commitment test, concierge delivery, paid pilot, landing-page conversion with qualified traffic, or manual workflow test. Submit the design for captain approval before outreach, publication, spend, or collection; desk research does not authorize the experiment.

Desk research can reject or reshape an idea but cannot prove demand. Without behavioral evidence, label demand `desk research only` and normally recommend `test further` or explain unusually strong contrary evidence.
