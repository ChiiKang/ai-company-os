# Product planning procedure

Produce an approved, budgeted plan before Builder acts.

1. **Outcome:** user, job, measurable behavior, and business/technical result.
2. **Evidence trace:** map each requirement to an assignment criterion, validation artifact, or explicit captain instruction. Mark assumptions; do not invent validation.
3. **Scope:** named end-to-end user journeys, assignment-specific functional behavior, in-scope capabilities, explicit exclusions/non-goals, and disclosed production deferrals with activation triggers.
4. **Acceptance:** measurable happy path and failure paths, accessibility, security/privacy constraints, reliability targets, scale/load assumptions, performance thresholds, endpoint behavior, and reproducible demonstration steps. Never use “works,” “production-ready,” “reliable,” or “scalable” without a metric and target.
5. **Architecture decisions:** simple alternatives, chosen option, trade-offs, reversibility, data flow, trust boundaries, and external dependencies.
6. **Work slices:** vertical milestones, tests/evidence for each, useful stop points, risks, and estimates.
7. **Resource policy:** project-local tooling, duration/iteration budget, zero-cost default, and separate approvals that may be needed.

The captain-approved plan identifier and its positive Builder budget enter the assignment contract. Plan approval does not authorize spending, credentials, destructive changes, production deployment, or out-of-scope construction.
